## 1. What DukaNest is

**DukaNest is a platform that gives a small business its own professional online
store** — a branded website with a product catalogue, cart, checkout, payments,
delivery fees, order tracking and stock management — all run from one dashboard or a
mobile app, with no coding and no developer.

The name says it plainly: _duka_ is the shop, and DukaNest is the home you give it.
Each store owner gets an address like `yourstore.dukanest.com` and a dashboard to run
the whole business. Hosting, security (SSL) and updates are included in the
subscription.

It is built specifically for **Kenyan sellers**: M-Pesa payments, delivery zones by
location, expense and profit tracking, and a mobile-first design because most owners
and shoppers are on a phone.

> **Say it like this:** DukaNest turns a WhatsApp-and-Instagram hustle into a real
> business with its own website — for about the price of a monthly data bundle.

— Jiji and Jumia rent you their audience and their rules; a developer charges
Ksh 50,000–130,000 and months of waiting. Close on price and the free trial: "You can
have your store live tonight."

## 3. Who it's for — and who it isn't

The sharper we are about this, the better our ads and conversations land. Our north
star:

> **One-sentence definition:** DukaNest is for small product sellers — those already
> selling on WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook, Jiji or Jumia, **and** entrepreneurs
> launching their first product business — who want their own affordable, professional
> online store.

### A strong fit

- Fashion, shoes, bags, thrift & boutique sellers
- Cosmetics, beauty and perfume sellers
- Electronics accessories and gadgets
- Home goods and baby products
- Small retail shops going online
- **Entrepreneurs starting out** — a first product line, a side hustle going
  full-time, a new brand that wants to look real from day one
- 1–3 people, owner-run, under ~200 orders/month
- Existing sellers doing Ksh 20,000–300,000/month
- Comfortable on a smartphone; on social media daily

### Not our audience

- Large enterprises and supermarkets
- Manufacturers and ERP users
- Developers and agencies
- Dropshipping "gurus"
- Service businesses and restaurants
- People with no product and no plan to sell one

> **For someone just starting:** Lead with the shortcut, not the pain: "Skip the messy
> WhatsApp phase entirely. Start with a real store, a proper checkout and M-Pesa from
> day one — and look established before you even have your tenth customer." The setup
> assistant and starter content help a brand-new seller fill the store fast.

### When they're most ready to buy

Orders are becoming hard to manage · the business is visibly growing · customers are
asking "do you have a website?" · they want to look professional · they're ready to
scale. Aim messaging at these moments.

### The belief we have to break

Almost all of them think _"websites are expensive and complicated."_ Every piece of
content should chip away at that — with price, with speed ("live in 20 minutes"), and
with "no coding, no developer."

## 4. The problem we solve

Sellers on WhatsApp and Instagram deal with the same friction every single day. Name
it back to them — recognition is what makes them lean in.

- **"Price?" all day.** Typing the same prices over and over, for hours a week.
- **Orders lost in chats.** Someone ordered three days ago — but in which conversation?
- **Delivery-fee arguments.** Working out charges by hand in DMs, then disputing them.
- **No stock visibility.** Selling the last piece twice, then apologising.
- **No trust.** New customers hesitate to send money to just a phone number.
- **No time to grow.** The whole day goes to replying to messages, not finding
  customers.
- **No numbers.** No idea which products actually make money.

> **The turn:** "What if all of that was just… handled? Customers see your prices and
> pictures on your own site, delivery fees show up automatically at checkout, and every
> order is recorded for you."

## 6. The built-in store assistant

Every DukaNest store comes with an **AI assistant built into the dashboard and the
app** — a chat helper that actually does work in the store, not just a help page. For
a solo owner it's like having a part-time shop assistant; for someone starting out it
removes the "I don't know where to begin" wall.

### What it does for the owner

- **Answers questions about their own numbers** — "How many orders this month?", "What
  was my revenue last week?", "How many pairs of shoes did I sell?"
- **Adds things for them** — walks them through adding a product, creates categories,
  sets up a sale or a delivery zone, right there in the chat.
- **Writes their marketing** — a WhatsApp/Instagram/SMS post about new arrivals, or a
  full blog article about their products, saved as a draft to review.
- **Gives business suggestions** — what categories a store like theirs should have,
  roughly what to charge for an item.
- **Tells them what to do next** — "What's left to set up?", "Am I ready to launch?" —
  using their real setup checklist.

> **Real example prompts (from the product):** "What should I do next?" · "How do I add
> a product?" · "How many orders do I have?" · "Write a social post about my new
> arrivals" · "Create a sale for my store" · "Write a blog post about caring for my
> products"

### There's also a setup assistant

When a new store is created, DukaNest can generate **starter content** — product
descriptions from the facts the owner types in, draft store pages, theme suggestions
based on their type of business, and marketing images. The owner reviews and edits
everything; nothing goes live on its own. This is what lets a brand-new seller get
from empty store to shareable link in one sitting.

> **How to talk about it:** "You're not doing this alone. DukaNest has a built-in
> assistant — ask it how you're doing, tell it to add a product, or ask it to write
> your next Instagram post. It's like a shop assistant that works inside your store."
> Keep it grounded: it helps you run the store, it doesn't run the business for you,
> and you approve everything it writes.

**Guardrails worth knowing:** the assistant only ever reads _that_ store's own data,
it never publishes anything without the owner clicking confirm, and drafts (sales,
blog posts) are saved as drafts. Usage has a monthly allowance per plan — see
Section 11.

## 7. Running the store from a phone

Most of our owners — and most of their shoppers — are on an Android phone. DukaNest is
built for that: the whole dashboard works properly on a small screen, and there's a
dedicated **DukaNest app for store owners**.

### What owners do from the app

- **Manage the catalogue** — add and edit products, use the phone camera to shoot
  product photos on the spot.
- **Handle orders** — see new orders, update statuses, work through fulfilment from
  anywhere.
- **Check the numbers** — revenue, best-sellers and store analytics in your pocket.
- **Get notified** — push notifications when a new order comes in, so nothing is
  missed.
- **Use the assistant** — the same built-in store assistant is in the app.

The web dashboard and the app share the same store — change something in one, it's
there in the other. The customer-facing storefront is mobile-friendly by default, so
shoppers get a clean experience on their phones without any extra work from the owner.

> **One-liner:** "Your shop runs from your phone. Add a product with your camera
> between customers, get a buzz when an order lands, check today's sales from the matatu
> home."

Before running app-download campaigns, check the current Google Play listing status
with the team — the owner app is rolling out, and a native iOS app and a separate
customer-facing app are still on the roadmap, not available yet.

## 10. vs. Jumia

Jumia is a large marketplace with real logistics reach — but as a third-party seller
you live by their commissions, their payout schedule, and their rules. DukaNest trades
their audience for your ownership, margin and control.

| Area | Jumia (as a seller) | With DukaNest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Commission | A cut of every sale, varying by category | No per-sale commission — flat monthly fee |
| Payouts | Paid on Jumia's schedule, after deductions | Paid straight to you via M-Pesa / your gateway |
| Pricing power | Pushed to discount; price wars with other sellers | You set prices; you run your own promotions |
| Brand & customer | Jumia owns the relationship and the data | You own the brand, the customer list and the data |
| Rules | Account can be suspended or penalised by their policy | Your store, your terms |
| Storefront | A template product page inside Jumia | A branded site you design and control |
| Marketing | Compete for placement inside Jumia | Drive your own traffic; keep every visitor |

> **Positioning:** "Jumia rents you their customers and takes a cut of every sale.
> DukaNest gives you a store where the customer — and the full price — is yours."

Don't quote specific Jumia commission percentages in ads — they change by category and
over time. "A cut of every sale" is safe and true.

## 12. Live in about 20 minutes

This sequence _is_ the counter-argument to "websites are complicated." Walk prospects
through it.

1. **Sign up & pick a theme** — register with Google or email, choose your store name
   and a ready-made design. ~2 minutes.
2. **Add your products** — upload photos, set prices, sort into categories — like
   posting on Instagram, but it's your own store.
3. **Set payments & delivery** — connect M-Pesa, set your delivery zones and fees.
   Done once.
4. **Share your link & sell** — put `yourstore.dukanest.com` on WhatsApp, Instagram
   and Facebook. Customers browse, order and pay through the site.

The **demo stores** page shows live examples before signing up. The free **one-month
trial** needs no credit card.

**"I already sell fine on WhatsApp / Instagram."**

**"What about Jumia / Jiji — isn't that easier?"**

## 16. Brand voice

Confident, plain-spoken, on the seller's side. We talk like a fellow hustler who
figured out a better way — not like software.

### Do

- Lead with _their_ pain, in their words ("Price?", lost orders)
- Use concrete numbers: Ksh 1,000, first month free, 20 minutes
- Say "your own store", "your brand", "your customers"
- Keep sentences short. One idea each.
- Name real features (delivery zones, stock alerts, M-Pesa)
- Position marketplaces as discovery, DukaNest as home
- Frame the assistant as a _helper_ the owner controls

### Don't

- Say "multi-tenant", "SaaS", "platform", "RLS" — ever, to customers
- Say the AI "runs your store" or "does it all for you" — it assists, the owner decides
- Promise features that are still "coming soon" (iOS app, customer app, custom domains)
- Quote competitors' exact commission or fee figures
- Attack sellers for using WhatsApp — meet them there
- Over-claim scale ("thousands of stores") without a source
- Sound corporate or apologetic

### Words that carry the brand

Your store · your brand · your customers · organised · professional · get found, then
bring them home · live tonight · no coding, no developer · less than a bundle.
